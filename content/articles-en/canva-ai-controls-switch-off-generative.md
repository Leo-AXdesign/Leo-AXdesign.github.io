Two menu entries are enough to make generative AI disappear from Canva. That is the news from Canva's AI Controls, announced on November 19 (US time). They are available to Free and Pro accounts at no extra cost.

It stands out because it is a setting for turning AI off, not on. For designers it matters whatever your view of AI, because these settings decide whether a delivered file carries an AI label and whether a library asset you picked was AI-made.

What follows cross-checks Engadget, 9to5Mac and Mumbrella, which quote Canva's announcement, plus a summary of Canva's help page that turned up in search. We did not open the app ourselves.

## Two settings

| Setting | What it does | Where (per press reports) |
|---|---|---|
| Generative AI switch | Hides supported generative AI features across Canva. Can be turned back on any time | Profile > Settings > Permissions > "Enable generative AI features" |
| Less AI-generated content | Reduces AI-made templates and elements in library search and recommendations. Existing designs are unchanged | Profile > Your account > AI personalization |

※ Sources: [9to5Mac, 2026-11-19](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/19/canva-introduces-new-controls-to-hide-generative-ai-features/), [Engadget, 2026-11-19](https://www.engadget.com/2321318/canva-will-let-users-switch-off-most-generative-ai/), [Mumbrella, 2026-11-20](https://mumbrella.com.au/canva-launches-new-ai-controls-for-creative-flexibility-945289) (checked 2026-11-20)

Menu names differ slightly between outlets. Engadget refers to the permissions section and also to the profile tab in the same piece. Labels may vary by region and app version, so look for "Permissions" and "AI personalization" on your own screen.

## Six tools go, two stay

Coverage varies on what the switch hides. 9to5Mac says only "supported" features and gives no list. One outlet, Martin Cid, did list them: Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Expand, Dream Lab, Canva Code and the image generator in Canva's chatbot. We could not confirm that list in Canva's own post, so the reliable method is to flip the switch and note what vanishes.

What stays is Background Remover and Magic Grab. Canva says they work only with what is already in a design, so they are not generative. They cut out or move existing pixels rather than creating new ones.

That has a practical side. Canva says neither tool adds an "AI-generated" label to file metadata. Some social platforms read that label, so a file made only with these two tools should not be flagged because of it. Whether a platform applies labels by other criteria is not covered in the reports. Output from generative tools may carry the label.

## "Show less" is not a filter

The second setting works differently. Canva says only a small part of its library is AI-made, mainly abstract backgrounds and illustrated graphics. Turning the setting on reduces such items in search and recommendations. Engadget and Martin Cid both stress it reduces them rather than blocks them.

One quoted line says Canva wants designs and illustrations made by real people to be easier to find. For client work this is useful: when a brief says "no generative AI sources," the step where you pick assets from the library is where they leak in. How accurately the setting recognizes AI-made items depends on how widely Canva has tagged them, and the help page summary also warned that some AI-generated content may still appear.

Before delivery, check the source label of what you used instead of trusting the setting alone.

## Five things to check on your own account

The announcement cannot answer these, but ten minutes in your account can.

- Turn the switch off and write down which tools disappear from the editor sidebar. Compare with the six above
- With it off, cut out a background with Background Remover, export, and see whether a social platform adds an AI label
- Search "abstract background" in the library and screenshot the first screen before and after the setting
- Check that generative elements in designs you already made are still there. Reports say existing designs do not change
- On a team account, admin controls are separate. See the next section

## Personal and team accounts take different paths

Canva says Business, Enterprise, Teams and Education plans already had admin permissions that set how a team uses AI. The new settings bring Free and Pro individuals to the same idea. If AI is on in a work or school account and that is a problem, the request goes to the admin, not to your personal settings. How the new settings overlap with team accounts was not described in the coverage.

## What is still open

- CNET reported, as relayed by Martin Cid, that the controls are not yet offered in India, the Philippines and Indonesia, with no timeline from Canva. We could not confirm availability in Korea
- Whether future generative features will sit behind the same switch or ship on by default is unsettled. Whether [Canva Craft](https://designrefs.com/articles/canva-craft-text-layout-image-model/), launched last month, is covered by the switch was not confirmed in the coverage
- The full list behind "supported features." Canva's help page did not open this time either, so we could not compare
- Whether the library setting also filters uploaded photos or third-party sources

## A switch to turn it off

Martin Cid also cites Canva's figure that its users have run AI tools more than 38 billion times (original not confirmed). The more people use AI, the more the option not to use it has to be designed in. Features that edit an existing design, like [Canva's brush edit](https://designrefs.com/articles/canva-brush-edit-existing-design/) from earlier this month, sit in that same flow.

How much AI shows up in a working file is a question clients may start asking first. Ten minutes today to write down what turns off and what stays on your account means you already have the answer when it comes.
